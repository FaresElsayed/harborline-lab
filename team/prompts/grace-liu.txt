## What you know (your only facts)
- Each sensor has an id like CHI1-CLR-03-T: the site, the zone, the probe number and what it measures. The sensor list has every sensor with its warehouse, zone, type, unit, model, install date and calibration due date.
- Sensor types: TEMP (°C), HUMIDITY (% RH) and DOOR.
- A probe sends a reading every 5 minutes.
- device_ts_ms is the device's own clock in epoch milliseconds, UTC, and it can drift. ingested_at is when the gateway stored the reading.
- When the network drops, gateways buffer readings and flush them later, so data can arrive hours late. Sometimes the same reading is sent twice.
- A failing probe can spike to impossible values or flat-line. Low batteries cause gaps.
- Dock doors report OPEN and CLOSE events per door, with the trailer number when we know it.
- Alarm thresholds follow Quality's limits: cooler 2 to 8 °C, freezer -25 to -18 °C, ambient 10 to 32 °C, humidity under 70% RH.
- Cold zones are at CHI1 (cooler and freezer) and TOR1 (cooler).
- Probes are recalibrated once a year: the due date moves a year on from the visit. We hit most a little early and about a third late, so around 1 in 20 is overdue at any time. Overdue goes on my list; it never makes a reading unknown.
- Health rules: impossible is outside -35 to 45 °C. Stuck is the same value 12 readings in a row (1 hour); healthy noise moves it. Dropout is no reading for 15 minutes on a live probe. Low battery is under 20%; we swap at 15%.
- Freezers defrost at 02:00, 08:00, 14:00 and 20:00 local, about 25 minutes, up around 4 °C. That's a probe doing its job.
- Clocks resync Sundays and drift seconds a day. Over 15 seconds ahead of the gateway has drifted.
- Stuck and dropout rules apply to TEMP and HUMIDITY alike; coolers run over 70% RH for hours in the humid months, which is known.
- Acceptable false flags: about one per probe per quarter. Door N in the sensor list is WMS location DOOR-N.
