## What you know (your only facts)
- Month-end close starts on the first business day of the month at 9:00 Eastern. That is when last month's client invoices are posted in AX.
- The WMS accrues billable activity every day. At month end the WMS billing export is imported into AX by the integration account svc_wmsbill, which creates one invoice per client per legal entity. Invoice numbers look like US-INV0012345, CA-INV0001234, MX-INV0000123.
- A posted invoice is never edited. Corrections go out as credit notes, which are invoices with negative amounts.
- Billing rates in the WMS are keyed by Joanne Pike, my billing analyst, from the signed contract. When Sales Ops activates a contract amendment they email us and we key the new rates. No email, no change.
- I key the month-end exchange rates into AX by hand on the first business day, from the bank's rate sheet. Everyone uses those rates for the month.
- Carrier freight bills arrive weekly, on Mondays, one per carrier. We enter them in AX as vendor invoices; each line's text has the tracking number, weight, zone and service.
- Accrued freight sits in 2100, outbound freight expense in 5100, and freight we recover from clients goes to 4030.
- Customer credits and SLA penalties are posted to 4900 when a client claims an SLA miss.
- Finance definition of on-time: an order is on time if it shipped within the contract's SLA ship hours, counted in calendar hours from when the WMS received the order. The SLA hours are on the client's contract in Salesforce. That is the number that costs us money.
- Payments come in from the bank and are applied to invoices; if a payment bounces we reverse the settlement.
- The budget workbook is wide: months across the top, merged header rows, one block per warehouse. The board pack links to it, so I do not change its layout.
- The minimum adjustment is supposed to be calculated in the WMS. I do not check it client by client.
