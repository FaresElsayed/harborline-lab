Answer key

Every project has something real to find in the data: a business story the simulator planted on purpose. This page explains each one, behind a spoiler gate. Open a project only after you have shipped its dashboards; finding the story yourself is the exercise.

Honor system

The gates only ask; they do not lock. The source of truth for the stories is answer_key/planted_stories.yaml, and every injected data defect is logged in answer_key/anomalies.csv. Both are spoilers in raw form.

“Month −10” below means ten months before the end of the history you built (the build date). The stories scale with your preset: with Small or Medium, stories older than your history may not appear.

Score your data-quality findings

This part is not a spoiler. Throughout the program you will find bad records: orphans, duplicates, impossible quantities, mojibake. Collect them in a CSV and the simulator tells you how many were real defects (precision) and how many of the injected defects you found (recall), per category.

The CSV needs a header and the columns system, object, record_key, and optionally category. system is one of wms, ax2012, iot, salesforce, carrier, edi, files; object is the table, Salesforce object or file name; record_key is the primary key value.

Save it in the simulator’s landing folder (the container sees it as /data/landing) and run, from the simulator folder:

docker compose exec app python -m harborline.tools.score_dq /data/landing/my_findings.csv

The defect rate is set in the Control Center (1 in 10,000 eligible records per category by default, from 1 in 100 to 1 in 100,000) and each category can be switched off. Turn the rate up while you build your checks, down to see whether they still find needles.

Planted stories by project

Project 1 · Data Platform Foundation1 story: the client crosswalk

Salesforce cannot be joined to the WMS for seven clients

Account.WMS_Owner_Code__c is blank for four active clients and mistyped for three: one in lower case, one with a trailing space, one with the letter O where the WMS code has a zero. Account executives type the code by hand at go-live, and nothing validates it.

Where it shows: a join from Salesforce accounts to wms.owner on the code drops seven active clients, and every later project that relies on it (revenue by client, the health score, the portal) silently loses them.

What good looks like: your Data Platform Health dashboard reports crosswalk coverage (clients matched in all three systems), the three typo cases are matched by a normalised key, the four blanks are listed for Dana to fix, and no downstream model drops a client without saying so.

Project 2 · Warehouse Performance4 stories: a picker outlier, NJ1 receiving, snow days, the LAX1 ramp

The ATL1 superstar is a data artefact

One ATL1 picker on shift B posts about 2.3 times the median picks per hour. The scans are batch-confirmed at the end of each tote, so all of a tote’s pick tasks complete within seconds of each other. It is not real productivity.

Where it shows: wms.task for that user has clusters of PICK completions a few seconds apart. A picks-per-hour metric built from completion times rewards it; one built from time between tote starts, or that flags implausible inter-pick gaps, does not. Shanice believes the number; Marcus knows the RF app allows it.

NJ1 dock-to-stock doubled six months ago

From month −6, NJ1’s dock-to-stock time roughly doubles, after a receiving position was cut on shift A to hit the budget. Luis mentions the cut if asked; he has not connected it to the metric.

Where it shows: trailer arrival (receipt_header.arrived_ts) to put-away (PUTAWAY tasks or ledger rows) at NJ1, by shift. A site-level monthly trend shows the step; splitting by shift shows where.

Snow days at CHI1 and NJ1

Snow days (from the real Open-Meteo archive when the build was online, synthetic otherwise) slow throughput and push shipments late at CHI1 and NJ1.

Where it shows: join daily weather for each site to on-time and throughput. The late days line up with snowfall, which is the context a scorecard needs before anyone blames a GM.

LAX1’s first eight weeks

LAX1 opened at month −17. For about eight weeks accuracy is poor and cycle times are long: a new-site ramp.

Where it shows: LAX1 cycle-count accuracy and order cycle time by week since opening. A good scorecard shows the ramp and lets the viewer exclude it from year-on-year comparisons.

The three definitions of on-time

Not planted in the data but in the people. Luis counts shipped by the requested ship date after the cutoff rule, in local time; Omar counts delivered by the date the client promised; Alan counts shipped within the contract’s SLA hours from receipt. The right outcome is all three, each named and owned, with one chosen as the headline.

Project 3 · Billing Assurance and AR4 stories and the payment behaviour of eleven clients

Glow Theory’s increase never reached billing

At month −10, Glow Theory (GLOWTH) reached its contract anniversary. The 4.5% increase was activated in Salesforce but never keyed into the WMS. From then on the WMS bills the old rates and AX invoices under-bill.

Where it shows: Rate_Card_Line__c rates on the active rate card against wms.billing_rate (and the rate on each billing_record) for GLOWTH. The gap is about 4.5% of every rated charge since month −10. Alan’s process explains it: no email, no change.

Lumen’s returns rate is a typo

From month −14, the RET-PROC (returns processing) rate for Lumen Activewear (LUMEN) is 0.33 instead of 3.30 in the WMS. Apparel returns are heavy, so the leak is large.

Where it shows: contract rate against WMS rate per charge code; a ratio of exactly 0.1 is the signature of a keying error.

PureForm Labs never paid its minimum

PureForm Labs (PUREFRM) stayed under its monthly minimum, but the minimum adjustment (MIN-ADJ) was never invoiced.

Where it shows: monthly billed total per client against the contract’s monthly minimum; MIN-ADJ lines are absent for PUREFRM in months where it was due.

AX month-end FX rates lag the ECB

Alan keys the month-end EUR/USD and GBP/USD rates by hand on the first business day. They differ from the ECB reference rate by 0.5 to 2.5%.

Where it shows: dbo.exchangerate (stored ×100) against Frankfurter’s ECB series for the same dates. The effect is on the EUR and GBP clients’ USD revenue and on realised FX gains and losses.

Project 4 · Freight Audit and Carrier Performance2 stories: a regional carrier overbills, a fuel surcharge lags

Velocity Regional Parcel overbills about 3% of shipments

On roughly 3% of its shipments, Velocity (VRPX) bills more than it should, in three ways: a billed weight above the package’s dimensional weight, duplicate lines for the same tracking number, and a residential surcharge on commercial addresses. Beth’s hunch is right.

Where it shows: parse the tracking number, weight and zone out of vendinvoicetrans.name on VRPX bills; join to wms.shipment and its cartons (actual weight and dimensions, residential flag on the order); price each line from the rate card. The three error types are separately countable and add up to a recoverable amount Beth can claim.

Marlowe applies fuel surcharges one week late

When the weekly diesel price moves, Marlowe (MRLF) applies the new surcharge percentage a week late.

Where it shows: the surcharge percentage implied by each Marlowe bill against the rate card’s fuel table applied to the EIA weekly diesel price. The billed percentage matches the previous week’s price, consistently. In a falling market that overcharges; in a rising one it undercharges.

Project 5 · Client 360EDI orders that vanish, and three churn stories

Some EDI orders never become WMS orders

About 0.4% of Apex Autoparts’ and Beacon Hardware’s 940s are rejected silently by the translator: no WMS order is created, yet the 997 still says accepted. Tomás believes the partner when the 997 is green.

Where it shows: reconcile 940 transactions (by the PO in W05) to wms.order_header.client_order_ref for EDI clients. The missing ones have an accepted 997 and no order. Resends that do arrive must not be counted twice.

Project 6 · Real-Time OperationsNo single story: the traps a correct CDC pipeline survives

Nothing is planted for Project 6. The test is whether the live board stays right while the WMS behaves like the operational system it is:

  • Hard deletes. Cancelled lines, zero-quantity inventory, voided cartons and corrected billing records disappear. Only change capture (or snapshot diffs) sees them.
  • order_line has no audit column. Updates to allocated, picked and shipped quantities are invisible to timestamp-based extraction.
  • The 180-day task purge. Thousands of deletes every night that are housekeeping, not cancellations. A live board that treats them as business events is wrong.
  • Label reprints change a shipment’s tracking number in place.
  • The repeated autumn hour in local timestamps, and site time zones in general.
  • Schema drift from the schema-drift scenario: a WMS release adds a column mid-stream.
  • Scenarios in the Control Center (peak surge, carrier outage, storm, late-data dump) should move the board the way the floor would see it, and the freshness alert should fire when the pipeline falls behind.
  • Replication slots. A stopped consumer grows WAL until the 8 GB cap invalidates the slot; your alerting should catch it first.
Project 7 · Cold Chain and Lot Traceability3 stories: summer excursions, a stuck sensor, a recall

Dock door 14 and hot afternoons

The CHI1 cooler zone goes out of range on hot summer afternoons when dock door 14 is left open for more than 20 minutes. Lots stored in the zone go on HOLD. Ayesha suspects open doors; this is the proof.

Where it shows: telemetry.dock_door_event open intervals for door 14 at CHI1, cooler readings in sensor_reading and the outdoor temperature from Open-Meteo, lined up in time; then the HOLD transactions on lots in that zone.

CHI1-CLR-03-T is stuck

For nine days sensor CHI1-CLR-03-T reports exactly 4.0 °C while the real temperature drifted. No excursion is detected, which is the hidden risk.

Where it shows: zero variance over a long window, against neighbouring probes in the same zone that move. A sensor-health view flags it; a pure threshold rule never will.

The Copper Kettle recall

Copper Kettle Foods (CPRKTL) recalls one lot. The trace must find every order and every ship-to that received it.

Where it shows: lot from receipt_line through inventory and inventory_txn to the PICK and SHIP transactions, orders and ship-to addresses. Watch for lots split across license plates and locations, and for returns of the same lot.

Project 8 · Client Portal and Executive ScorecardNo new story: what the final scorecard and portal must get right

Project 8 brings everything together. A correct result:

  • Executive scorecard: shows the churned clients and the Solstice risk, Oakline’s DSO, the revenue recovered from the billing and freight findings, LAX1 after its ramp, and on-time with its definition stated.
  • Client portal: each client sees only its own rows. Test it with the crosswalk cases from Project 1: a client whose Salesforce code was mistyped must neither lose its data nor see another client’s.
  • Personal data: ship-to names, addresses, phones and emails are masked for every role that does not need them, and access is audited.
