The story

Harborline stores inventory for about sixty brands in eight warehouses across the United States, Canada and Mexico, picks and packs their orders, ships them with a dozen carriers and handles their returns. It runs on a legacy warehouse system, Microsoft Dynamics AX 2012 for finance and Salesforce for sales, plus carrier feeds, temperature sensors and EDI with retail partners. Every department reports from its own spreadsheets, and the numbers disagree.

Sam Okafor, the Director of IT, hired you to fix that. Over about 32 weeks you will build a data platform on Databricks or Snowflake and deliver one project per department, each ending in dashboards: platform health, warehouse performance, billing assurance, freight audit, client 360, real-time operations, cold chain, and finally a client portal and the executive scorecard.

The data is simulated, but it behaves like the real thing. Orders arrive, change and get cancelled; rows are physically deleted; timestamps are in local time; a carrier quietly overbills; a sensor gets stuck. Each project has a real business story hidden in the data, and the people who own the systems know the rules but not the answers.

Quick start

One setup file does the installing, on Windows or Mac. It skips what you already have and only stops when it needs you. The details are here.

Extract the zip. On Windows, double-click SETUP-WINDOWS.cmd (More info, then Run anyway if Windows warns you). On a Mac, open Terminal, type bash , drag SETUP-MAC.command into the window and press Return. It finishes by setting up your team chats. If it stops, paste its report to Kevin O’Hara, do what he says and run it again.

The setup file walks you through one saved AI chat per colleague; run it again for anyone you skipped. The Team page shows who does what and what to ask them. Start with Kevin O’Hara (IT help) and Nadia Petrov (your team lead).
